## Tarav object and record boundaries

The discussion continued the goal that meaningful, independently serializable Tarav data should derive from `TaravObject`. An audit of Diagram prompted a separate treatment for structural values embedded inside those objects.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 3932f86e0c124a0275e615c7483c9e41234e7ba19a3a6a4cfd0884ac7ec6164a -->
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb ef32ab6ad0591ab8c615b573e887dfa91008f6d0e06dc78984d63b9e4ba67b23 -->

The settled direction keeps `TaravObject.RecordInterface` as the root of every Tarav record interface. Reusable embedded interfaces such as `SupportMemberInterface` should derive directly from it; they gain the common record contract without becoming registered `TaravObject` types.

An intermediate design introduced a new top-level `RecordInterface` above `TaravObject.RecordInterface`. The later decision superseded that extra root in favor of the existing nested root.

## Best-effort mechanics and inspectable failure

The Breadcrumb discussion established a general Tarav principle: use small, best-effort mechanics such as locking and atomic replacement to reduce common failures, while preserving enough evidence for agents to understand and repair ill states. Tarav detects suspicious states and surfaces POIs; workflows do not need automatic recovery or perfect consistency.
